Workflow

From a full SD card to the keepers

Open a folder and it is indexed in place. Every photo gets a score in the background, and you work through six workspaces: Browse, Review, Similarity, Enhance & Export, Stats, and Debug.

Browse and score

A grid or list of the whole folder with filename search, a minimum AI score, star filters, and sorting by score, name, or date. The Inspector explains each score: sharpness, exposure, contrast, clipping, and, with the optional AI runtime, image-quality model metrics. Override any score by hand.

Review one frame at a time

A large preview with zoom, star ratings, and a filmstrip. Move with H J K L, press Q to queue a photo for export, and Space to enlarge it. AI Rate Folder scores everything in one go.

Collapse the near-duplicates

Similarity groups burst shots and near-duplicates with perceptual hashes, then CLIP and FAISS when they are installed. Each group suggests a keeper and marks the rest. Removing photos only takes them out of the workspace; the files stay on disk and can be restored.

  • Local

    Photos are indexed in place. Recipes live in a local database, and export never overwrites an original.

  • Batch

    Copy and paste adjustments by group, apply them to the whole queue, or save a look as a preset.

  • Export

    sRGB JPEG or 16-bit sRGB TIFF with an embedded ICC profile. Capture metadata is kept; GPS is not copied.

Editing

A RAW editor that starts in the right place

The camera's own tone curve

DJI DNGs embed a ProfileToneCurve, the same curve the in-camera JPEG and Lightroom use. The editor develops the RAW at 16 bits with camera white balance, lifts it +0.6 EV, and applies that curve without shifting hue. At zero, a photo already looks like the camera's JPEG instead of flat and gray.

Auto

Conservative, per-photo suggestions that land on the regular sliders. Night scenes stay dark, crushed shadows and near-clipped highlights get attention, and an Auto strength slider scales the result.

The Tone/Color pad

One square, two axes: More Tone ↔ Softer vertically, Muted ↔ Vivid horizontally. Dragging it moves Contrast, Black point, Shadows, Highlights, Brilliance, Vibrance, and Saturation together, in directions worked out from each photo's histogram and skin and sky detection. Deep shadows aren't pushed deeper, highlights near clipping are protected, and skies get gentler saturation. It moves the real sliders, so you can fine-tune anything afterwards.

Everything else you reach for

Install

Get it running

1. Download

Get the latest build from the releases page. On macOS (Apple Silicon), drag AI Photo Culling.app from the DMG into Applications. On Windows 10/11 (x64), double-click the .msi; it installs for your user without admin rights. Each release publishes SHA256SUMS.txt.

2. Allow it to open

Builds aren't code signed yet. On macOS, open the app once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. If macOS says the app is damaged, run:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine \
  "/Applications/AI Photo Culling.app"

On Windows, if SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC", click More info → Run anyway.

3. Stay current

The app checks GitHub Releases on launch, or via Check for Updates... (in the app menu on macOS, under Help on Windows). Updates are verified against SHA256SUMS.txt and installed on restart or quit. On macOS keep the app in /Applications for this to work. AI_CULLING_DISABLE_UPDATES=1 turns it off.
